UK take-home pay calculator 2026/27

Enter your salary to estimate take-home pay after income tax, employee National Insurance and pension for the 2026/27 tax year.

  • Free, no sign-up
  • Runs privately in your browser
  • Updated for 2026/27

2026/27 tax year rates for England, Wales and Northern Ireland: £12,570 personal allowance, 20% / 40% / 45% income tax, 8% / 2% employee National Insurance. Pension is treated as taken before tax. Doesn’t include student loans, Scottish rates or tax code adjustments.

How to use the UK take-home pay calculator

  1. Enter your gross annual salary before tax.
  2. Add your pension contribution as a percentage of salary, if you pay into a workplace pension.
  3. Read your take-home pay per year, month and week, and the income tax and National Insurance you pay.

2026/27 rates used

The calculator uses income tax rates for England, Wales and Northern Ireland:

  • Personal allowance: £12,570 tax-free. It is reduced by £1 for every £2 of income over £100,000.
  • Basic rate 20%: the next £37,700 of taxable income (up to £50,270 of salary).
  • Higher rate 40%: up to £125,140.
  • Additional rate 45%: above £125,140.

Employee Class 1 National Insurance is 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above that.

What the estimate leaves out

This is a simplified estimate. It does not include Scottish income tax bands, student or postgraduate loan repayments, salary sacrifice, benefits in kind, emergency tax codes or other payroll deductions. Pension contributions are treated as a net pay arrangement, reducing income tax but not National Insurance. Check your payslip or the official HMRC tools for exact figures.

Frequently asked questions

How much is £30,000 after tax in the UK?

In 2026/27, a £30,000 salary in England, Wales or Northern Ireland gives take-home pay of about £25,119.60 a year, or £2,093.30 a month, with no pension contribution.

What is the tax-free personal allowance for 2026/27?

£12,570. It starts to reduce once income goes above £100,000 and is fully removed at £125,140.

Does this calculator work for Scotland?

Not yet. Scotland has different income tax bands, so the results will not match a Scottish payslip. National Insurance is the same across the UK.

Does my pension reduce my tax?

Yes. In a net pay arrangement, pension contributions are taken before income tax is calculated, so you pay less tax. National Insurance is still charged on your full salary.
