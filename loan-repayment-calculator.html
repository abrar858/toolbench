    Loan repayment calculator

    Work out the monthly payment and total interest on a loan, car finance deal or mortgage, see the repayment schedule, and find out how much overpaying, paying a lump sum or settling early would save.

    • Free, no sign-up
    • Runs privately in your browser
    • Updated for 2026/27
    Pay off early: lump sum or full settlement

    A lump sum lowers the balance and keeps the same monthly payment, so the loan ends sooner. Settling estimates what you'd still owe after that month's payment, and what you'd avoid paying.

    How to use the loan repayment calculator

    1. Enter the amount you want to borrow.
    2. Enter the interest rate (APR).
    3. Enter the term in years, plus any extra months.
    4. Read the monthly payment, total repaid and total interest.
    5. To pay off early, open Pay off early: lump sum or full settlement, and enter a lump sum or the month you want to settle after.

    How repayments are calculated

    The calculator uses the standard repayment loan formula, where each fixed monthly payment covers that month's interest and pays off some of the balance. Monthly payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), where P is the amount borrowed, r is the APR divided by 12 and n is the number of months.

    Worked examples

    • Borrowing £10,000 at 6.9% over 5 years costs about £197.54 a month. You repay £11,852.43 in total, of which £1,852.43 is interest.
    • A £200,000 mortgage at 4.5% over 25 years costs about £1,111.66 a month on a repayment basis, with about £133,499 of interest over the full term.
    • A £30,000 loan at 6.9% over 10 years costs about £346.78 a month, with £11,613.75 of interest in total.
    • A 0% deal of £5,000 over 24 months is £208.33 a month.

    Shorter or longer term?

    A longer term lowers the monthly payment but usually increases the total interest. Compare both figures before you choose, and check for arrangement fees, which this estimate does not include. Your lender's figures may differ slightly because of how they calculate interest.

    Repayment schedule and overpayments

    Open Repayment schedule by year to see how much of each year's payments goes on interest and how much reduces what you owe, and download the month-by-month schedule as a CSV file for Excel or Google Sheets. Enter an amount in Overpay each month to see how much sooner the loan is cleared and how much interest you save. For example, overpaying £100 a month on £10,000 at 6.9% over 5 years clears it in 38 months instead of 60. Check whether your lender charges early repayment fees first.

    Lump sums and settling early

    Open Pay off early to see the effect of a one-off payment, such as a bonus or savings, or of clearing the loan completely.

    • Lump sum: paying £2,000 after month 12 on £10,000 at 6.9% over 5 years clears the loan in 4 years instead of 5 and saves £544.78 in interest. Your monthly payment stays the same; the loan just ends sooner.
    • Settling in full: after month 24 of the same loan, about £6,407.12 is left to repay. With up to 58 days of extra interest, which lenders can usually add, the settlement would be about £6,477.37, and you would still save at least £634.09 of interest.

    These are estimates. Your lender's settlement figure is the exact amount, and you can ask for it at any time.

    How the term changes the cost

    £10,000 at 6.9% APRMonthlyTotal interest
    3 years£308.31£1,099.30
    5 years£197.54£1,852.43
    7 years£150.44£2,636.83

    Stretching the same loan from 3 to 7 years halves the monthly payment but more than doubles the interest. The same pattern applies to mortgages: £200,000 at 4.5% costs £1,111.66 a month over 25 years and £1,013.37 over 30 years, but the extra 5 years add about £31,300 of interest.

    APR and representative APR

    APR includes interest and compulsory fees, so it's the fairest way to compare loans. A representative APR in an advert only has to be offered to at least 51% of successful applicants; the rate you're offered depends on your credit record and can be higher. Use the rate in your actual quote for the most accurate figures.

    Paying off early

    Overpaying reduces the balance that interest is charged on, which is why even small regular overpayments shorten a loan. For personal loans covered by the Consumer Credit Act you have the right to repay early, though the lender can charge a limited fee. Many mortgages allow overpayments of up to 10% a year without a charge during a fixed rate. Check your agreement before overpaying.

    To clear a personal loan completely, ask the lender for a settlement figure. Under the Consumer Credit (Early Settlement) Regulations 2004 you get a rebate of the interest you haven't yet been charged, but the lender can usually add up to 58 days of interest, and some fixed-rate loans also carry a small fee. Mortgages and some other loans follow their own terms.

    Frequently asked questions

    How much do overpayments save?

    Enter your monthly overpayment and the calculator shows how many months sooner the loan ends and the interest saved, before any early repayment charges.

    How do I work out an early settlement figure?

    Open Pay off early and enter the month you want to settle after. The calculator shows the balance left and an estimate including up to 58 days of extra interest. Ask your lender for the exact settlement figure.

    Is it worth paying off a loan early?

    It usually saves interest, because interest is charged on a smaller balance for less time. Check for an early repayment fee, and keep an emergency fund before using savings to clear a loan.

    How much is a £10,000 loan per month?

    At 6.9% APR over 5 years, about £197.54 a month. Change the rate and term to see your own figures.

    Can I use this as a mortgage calculator?

    Yes, for a repayment mortgage. Enter the mortgage amount, rate and term. It does not model interest-only mortgages, rate changes after a fixed period or fees.

    What is APR?

    The annual percentage rate is the yearly cost of borrowing, including interest and some compulsory fees, which makes deals easier to compare.

    Is a longer loan term better?

    It lowers the monthly payment but increases the total interest. Choose the shortest term whose monthly payment you can comfortably afford.
