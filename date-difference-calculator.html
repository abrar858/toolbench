    Date difference calculator

    Count calendar days, weeks and UK working days between two dates, or add working days to a date, with bank holidays for each UK nation.

    • Free, no sign-up
    • Runs privately in your browser
    • Instant results

    Add or subtract days

    Calendar totals include weekends. The end date is excluded unless inclusive counting is selected. Bank holidays come from GOV.UK and cover 2019 to 2028. Time zones and daylight saving do not change the result.

    How to use the date difference calculator

    1. Choose a start date and an end date.
    2. Tick 'Include both start and end dates' if both days should count.
    3. Read the total days, weeks and weekdays. Use 'Swap dates' if you entered them the wrong way round.

    Inclusive or exclusive counting

    By default the end date is not counted, which matches how most people count time between dates: from Monday to Friday is 4 days. If you need to count both days, for example the days of a holiday you will be away, tick the inclusive option and Monday to Friday becomes 5 days.

    Weekdays and bank holidays

    Weekdays exclude Saturdays and Sundays but still include bank holidays, so treat the figure as a guide rather than a working-day total. Time zones and clock changes do not affect the result.

    Worked example

    From 1 January 2026 to 31 March 2026 is 89 days, or 12 weeks and 5 days, when the end date is excluded. With inclusive counting it is 90 days.

    Common uses include counting down to a holiday or wedding, working out a notice period or probation end date, checking how long a contract or tenancy runs, tracking a pregnancy or training plan in weeks, and checking deadlines for refunds and returns.

    Remember that legal and contractual deadlines can have their own rules for counting days, such as excluding bank holidays. Check the wording that applies to you.

    UK bank holidays and working days

    Choose England and Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland to skip that nation's bank holidays as well as weekends. Bank holiday dates come from GOV.UK and cover 2019 to 2028. Use Add or subtract days for deadlines: for example, 10 working days after Monday 21 December 2026 in England is Thursday 7 January 2027, because Christmas Day, the 28 December substitute holiday and New Year's Day are skipped.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I count working days excluding bank holidays?

    Enter both dates and choose your nation under ‘Leave out UK bank holidays’. The result shows working days, bank holidays skipped and weekend days.

    How many days are there between two dates?

    Enter both dates and the calculator shows the number of calendar days, excluding the end date unless you choose inclusive counting.

    Does it count weekends?

    The calendar day total includes weekends. A separate weekday total excludes Saturdays and Sundays.

    Does it handle leap years?

    Yes. Real calendar dates are used, so 29 February is counted in leap years.
