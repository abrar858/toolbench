    Early loan and car finance settlement calculator

    Estimate how much it would cost to pay off a personal loan, or PCP or hire purchase car finance, in full now, the interest you'd save, and the most a lender could add under the UK early settlement rules.

    • Free, no sign-up
    • Runs privately in your browser
    • Updated for 2026/27

    By Bisma Zainab, developer of ToolsBravo · Figures checked against official sources · Reviewed 22 November 2026

    Assumes you ask just after a monthly payment, so no other payment falls due before you settle. Your lender's settlement statement gives the exact figure.

    How to use the early loan settlement calculator

    1. Choose whether you know the amount borrowed and the term, or your monthly payment and the number of payments left (both are on your statement).
    2. Enter the APR from your loan agreement.
    3. For PCP car finance, enter the final balloon payment (the optional final payment, sometimes called the GFV). Leave it at 0 for a loan or hire purchase.
    4. Leave the settlement date at 28 days, the date lenders use after they receive your request.
    5. Read the settlement figure, the interest saved and the highest figure a lender could ask for.
    6. Open If you settle later to compare settling after more payments.

    How the settlement figure is worked out

    Under the Consumer Credit (Early Settlement) Regulations 2004, you don't pay the interest that would have been charged after you settle. The lender works out what you owe by discounting your remaining payments at the loan's APR back to the settlement date, which is 28 days after it receives your request. That is the same as the balance you owe now plus interest up to the settlement date.

    When more than a year of the loan is left, the rules let the lender work out the rebate as if you settled one month later, which adds about a month's interest. That's why you'll often read that lenders can add "up to 58 days" of interest: 28 days plus a month.

    Early repayment compensation

    For agreements made on or after 1 February 2011, section 95A of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 lets a lender claim compensation when more than £8,000 is repaid early in any 12 months. It is capped at 1% of the amount repaid if more than a year of the loan is left, or 0.5% if a year or less is left, and never more than the interest you would otherwise have paid. Many lenders don't charge it. Loans secured on your home and overdrafts are covered by different rules.

    Worked example

    You borrowed £15,000 at 9.9% APR over 5 years and have made 24 payments of £314.86, so 36 are left (£11,335.01 still to pay).

    • Settling 28 days after you ask costs about £9,904.02, saving £1,430.99 of interest.
    • If the lender uses the one-month deferral, the figure rises to about £9,979.95.
    • Because more than £8,000 is repaid with more than a year left, the lender could also claim up to 1%, about £99.04.
    • Even with both, you'd pay at most about £10,078.99 and save at least £1,256.02.

    Car finance: PCP and hire purchase

    Car finance taken out as hire purchase (HP) or personal contract purchase (PCP) follows the same early settlement rules as a loan: you get a rebate of the interest for the time you no longer need the credit. The difference with PCP is the large final balloon payment at the end. To own the car early you settle the remaining monthly payments and the balloon, discounted at the APR.

    Example: a £20,000 PCP at 7.9% APR over 48 months with an £8,000 final payment costs £341.71 a month. After 24 payments, £16,201.10 is still due (24 × £341.71 plus £8,000). Settling 28 days after you ask costs about £14,539.89, saving £1,661.21. With the one-month deferral and 1% compensation, the most you'd pay is about £14,775.71, still saving £1,425.39.

    If you don't want to keep the car, you have other options. Under the Consumer Credit Act you can end an HP or PCP agreement by voluntary termination once you've paid half the total amount payable (including any deposit and fees), and hand the car back. With PCP you can also hand the car back at the end instead of paying the balloon, or part-exchange it. Settling only makes sense if you want to own the car, or to sell it yourself.

    Getting your exact figure

    Ask your lender for a settlement statement. You can ask at any time, in writing or often online, and the lender must send it within 7 days. It shows the exact amount and the date it's valid until. Pay by that date to clear the loan, and keep the confirmation that the account is closed.

    Before you settle, keep an emergency fund, and compare the interest saved with what the money would earn in savings. If you can't clear it all, a part payment reduces the interest too: see the loan repayment calculator.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is an early loan settlement figure calculated?

    Your remaining payments are discounted at the loan's APR to the settlement date, 28 days after your request. In practice it's the balance you owe plus interest to that date, sometimes plus about one more month's interest when more than a year is left.

    How much will I save by paying off my loan early?

    The difference between the payments you still have to make and the settlement figure. For £15,000 at 9.9% APR over 5 years, settling after 24 payments saves about £1,256 to £1,431.

    Can a lender charge a fee for paying off a loan early?

    For agreements from 1 February 2011, only if more than £8,000 is repaid early in 12 months: up to 1% of the amount, or 0.5% with a year or less left, and never more than the interest you'd have paid.

    How long does a settlement figure last?

    The settlement statement gives a date, usually 28 days after your request. Pay by then and the loan is cleared for the amount shown.

    Does this work for car finance?

    Yes. Hire purchase and PCP follow the same early settlement rules. For PCP, enter the final balloon payment so it's included in the settlement figure. For a standard loan used to buy a car, leave the balloon at 0.

    How do I work out a PCP settlement figure?

    Enter the amount financed, APR, term, payments made and the final balloon payment. The settlement is your remaining monthly payments plus the balloon, discounted at the APR to 28 days after your request. For £20,000 at 7.9% over 48 months with an £8,000 balloon, settling after 24 payments costs about £14,540.

    Can I hand the car back instead of settling?

    Often, yes. HP and PCP agreements can be ended by voluntary termination once you've paid half the total amount payable, including any deposit. With PCP you can also return the car at the end instead of paying the balloon.

    Is paying a loan off early bad for my credit score?

    No. A loan marked as settled is a positive record. The account will close, which can slightly change your credit mix, but repaying in full is not a mark against you.
