    Strong password generator

    Create strong random passwords, or memorable passphrases made of random words, with a clear strength rating. Nothing leaves your device.

    • Free, no sign-up
    • Runs privately in your browser
    • Instant results

    Words come from the EFF long word list (7,776 words, CC BY 3.0).

    How to use the password generator

    1. Drag the slider to choose a length. 16 characters or more is a good default.
    2. Choose which characters to include: capitals, small letters, numbers and symbols.
    3. Tick 'Exclude look-alike characters' if you will need to read or type the password by hand.
    4. Copy the password straight into your password manager.

    How the passwords are made

    Passwords are built with your browser's cryptographic random number generator (the Web Crypto API), the same kind of randomness used for encryption keys. They are created on your device and are not stored or sent to a server.

    What makes a password strong

    Length matters most. Each extra character multiplies the number of possible passwords, so a 16-character password using all character types is far harder to guess than an 8-character one. Use a different password for every account and keep them in a password manager. The UK National Cyber Security Centre also suggests three random words as a memorable option for passwords you have to remember.

    Passphrases: random words

    Choose Passphrase to join random words from the EFF long word list, for example Unheard-Spherical-Recharger-36-Unhelpful. Four random words give about 52 bits of randomness, and adding a number and a fifth word takes it past 60 bits. Passphrases are far easier to type and remember than random characters, which makes them a good choice for the few passwords you can't keep in a password manager. The strength meter estimates bits of randomness: under 40 is weak, 60 or more is strong.

    How strong is strong enough?

    PasswordRandomness
    8 random characters (all types)about 51 bits
    12 random charactersabout 77 bits
    16 random charactersabout 103 bits
    4 random wordsabout 52 bits
    6 random wordsabout 78 bits

    Each extra bit doubles the number of guesses needed. For accounts that matter, aim for 60 bits or more, and use a different password for every site.

    How passwords are actually stolen

    Most accounts aren't broken into by guessing. Attackers take email and password pairs leaked from one website and try them on others, which only works if you reuse passwords. Phishing emails and fake login pages are the other big cause. A unique password per site, kept in a password manager, plus two-step verification, protects against nearly all of this. You can check whether your email address appears in known leaks at Have I Been Pwned.

    Following NCSC advice

    The UK National Cyber Security Centre recommends three random words for passwords you need to remember, a password manager for everything else, and turning on two-step verification for email and banking. Use passphrase mode for the first, and random characters for passwords your manager stores.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is a passphrase?

    A password made of several random words, such as Railcar-Monastery-Blurry-20. Random words chosen by a computer are much harder to guess than words people pick themselves.

    How long should a password be?

    Aim for at least 12 to 16 characters for important accounts, and use a password manager so you do not need to remember them.

    Is it safe to use an online password generator?

    This generator runs entirely in your browser using secure randomness, and the password is never uploaded. Close the page after copying it.

    Should I include symbols?

    Symbols add strength, but some sites limit which symbols are allowed. If a site rejects the password, turn symbols off and increase the length instead.

    Should I use a password manager?

    Yes, for most people. The ones built into browsers and phones, from Apple, Google and Microsoft, are free and sync across your devices, so every site can have its own long random password.
