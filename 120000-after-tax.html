    £120,000 after tax in the UK

    On a £120,000 salary you take home £76,157.40 a year, £6,346.45 a month or £1,464.57 a week in the 2026/27 tax year.

    By Bisma Zainab, developer of ToolsBravo · Figures checked against official sources · Reviewed 22 November 2026

    Figures are for England, Wales and Northern Ireland with a standard 1257L tax code, no pension contribution and no student loan. You keep 63.5% of your salary; 36.5% goes on income tax and National Insurance.

    How the tax is worked out

    • Tax-free personal allowance: £2,570.
    • Basic rate at 20% on £37,700: £7,540.00.
    • Higher rate at 40% on £79,730: £31,892.00.
    • National Insurance at 8% on £37,700 of earnings between £12,570 and £50,270: £3,016.00.
    • National Insurance at 2% on £69,730 above £50,270: £1,394.60.

    A pay rise of £100 at this level would leave you about £38.00 better off, because the next pound is taxed at a combined 62%.

    Because income is over £100,000, the personal allowance is reduced by £1 for every £2 above that level. At £120,000 the allowance is £2,570. Between £100,000 and £125,140 this creates an effective income tax rate of 60%, plus 2% National Insurance. Pension contributions that bring income back below £100,000 can restore the allowance.

    With a 5% workplace pension

    Paying 5% of salary (£6,000.00 a year) into a net pay pension scheme reduces income tax to £35,832.00 and leaves take-home pay of £73,757.40 a year, or £6,146.45 a month. The pension costs you £2,400.00 a year in take-home pay because tax relief makes up the rest. Salary sacrifice schemes also save National Insurance.

    Hourly and daily rate

    On a 37.5-hour week, £120,000 works out at about £61.54 an hour before tax, or £39.06 an hour after tax and National Insurance. Over 260 working days a year, that's £461.54 a day before tax and £292.91 a day after.

    £120,000 after tax with a student loan

    Student loan repayments are 9% of earnings above your plan's threshold (6% for a postgraduate loan), taken through payroll. On Plan 2, the most common plan for students from England who started university between 2012 and 2023, you repay £8,155.35 a year (£679.61 a month), so take-home pay falls to £5,666.84 a month.

    £120,000 after tax in Scotland

    Scotland sets its own income tax bands, and National Insurance is the same across the UK. On £120,000 a Scottish taxpayer pays £44,232.05 income tax and takes home £71,357.35 a year, or £5,946.45 a month. That's £4,800.05 a year less than in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, with the top slice of income in the Scottish advanced rate band.

    Frequently asked questions

    How much is £120,000 a month after tax?

    In the 2026/27 tax year, £120,000 a year is £6,346.45 a month after income tax and National Insurance, assuming a standard tax code, no pension contribution and no student loan, in England, Wales or Northern Ireland.

    What tax band is a £120,000 salary in?

    £120,000 falls in the higher rate (40%) band. Each extra £1 earned is taxed at a combined rate of about 62% including National Insurance.

    How much is £120,000 an hour?

    At 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, £120,000 is about £61.54 an hour before tax and £39.06 an hour after tax and National Insurance.

    How much tax do I pay on £120,000?

    £39,432.00 income tax and £4,410.60 employee National Insurance a year: £43,842.60 in total, or 36.5% of your salary.

    How much is £120,000 after tax in Scotland?

    In 2026/27, £71,357.35 a year or £5,946.45 a month, because Scotland has its own income tax bands. National Insurance is the same across the UK.

    How much is £120,000 after tax with a student loan?

    On Plan 2, £5,666.84 a month after repaying £679.61 a month. Plan 1, Plan 4, Plan 5 and postgraduate loans have different thresholds: see the table above.

    How much is £120,000 a day?

    Over 260 working days a year, £461.54 a day before tax and £292.91 a day after income tax and National Insurance.
