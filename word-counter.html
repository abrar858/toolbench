    Word counter and character counter

    Paste or type text to count words, characters, sentences and paragraphs, and see how long it takes to read or say out loud.

    • Free, no sign-up
    • Runs privately in your browser
    • Instant results

    How to use the word counter

    1. Type or paste your text into the box.
    2. Pick a reading speed and speaking speed if the standard estimates do not suit you.
    3. Read the live counts: words, characters, characters without spaces, sentences, paragraphs, lines, bytes, reading time and speaking time.

    What it counts

    A word is any run of characters separated by spaces or line breaks. Characters are counted the way people see them, so an emoji or an accented letter counts as one. Sentences end with a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark, and paragraphs are separated by a blank line.

    Reading and speaking time

    Reading time assumes 200 words per minute and speaking time 130 words per minute by default. A 1,000-word essay takes about 5 minutes to read and just under 8 minutes to present. Change the speeds for slower or faster readers.

    Common word limits

    Use the counter to check essays and assignments, personal statements, cover letters, blog posts, product descriptions and speeches. Different software can count hyphenated words, numbers and symbols differently, so check your institution's rules when an exact limit matters.

    Common limits

    WhereLimit
    Post on X (Twitter)280 characters

    UCAS personal statementUp to 4,000 characters including spaces (check UCAS for the current format)
    A 5-minute speechAbout 650 words at 130 words a minute

    Why counts differ between apps

    Word processors don't all count the same way. Microsoft Word treats a hyphenated word like ‘well-known’ as one word, and so does this counter. Some tools count numbers, symbols or URLs differently, and some count an em dash with spaces around it as a word. If you have a strict limit, use the counter your teacher, publisher or form uses, and keep a small margin.

    Cutting words without losing meaning

    • Remove filler such as ‘very’, ‘really’, ‘in order to’ (use ‘to’) and ‘due to the fact that’ (use ‘because’).
    • Turn passive sentences active: ‘the report was written by the team’ becomes ‘the team wrote the report’.
    • Cut repeated points, and introductions that only say what you're about to say.

    Writing to a word count

    Planning a word budget before you start saves cutting later. For a 2,000-word essay, a common split is about 200 words of introduction, three or four main sections of 400 to 450 words each, and about 200 words of conclusion. Many universities allow a margin, often 10% either way, and leave out references and the bibliography from the count, but rules vary between courses, so check your module handbook. Paste each section in as you go to keep track.

    Frequently asked questions

    How many words is a 5-minute speech?

    At a typical speaking pace of 130 words per minute, a 5-minute speech is about 650 words.

    Does the word counter count characters with spaces?

    Yes. It shows characters with spaces and without spaces.

    Is my text saved or uploaded?

    No. Counting happens in your browser. Nothing you type is uploaded or stored.

    How many words is 4,000 characters?

    Roughly 550 to 700 words, depending on how long your words are. Paste your text to see the exact character count.
