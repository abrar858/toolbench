    How to convert CSV to Excel

    1. Choose one or more CSV files.
    2. Leave the separator on Detect automatically, or pick comma, semicolon, tab or pipe.
    3. Choose one Excel file per CSV, or one workbook with a sheet for each CSV.
    4. Press Convert to Excel and download.

    Why CSV files open badly in Excel

    Excel guesses how to read a CSV and often gets it wrong: everything lands in one column when the file uses semicolons, accented letters turn into odd symbols, and codes like 00123 lose their leading zeros. Converting to .xlsx fixes the structure once, so the file opens correctly everywhere.

    Leading zeros, phone numbers and dates

    Values are only turned into numbers when nothing would be lost. Product codes like 00123, phone numbers like +447700900123 and IDs longer than 15 digits stay exactly as written, while amounts like 3.50 become real numbers you can add up, keeping their decimal places. Dates written as 2026-01-05 become real dates. Dates with slashes, like 05/01/2026, stay as text, because they could mean 5 January or 1 May depending on where the file came from; convert them in Excel with Data → Text to Columns → Date: DMY. Tick Keep every value as text if you want nothing converted at all.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I convert CSV to Excel?

    Choose the CSV file, press Convert to Excel and open the downloaded .xlsx file.

    My CSV uses semicolons. Will it work?

    Yes. The separator is detected automatically, or you can choose semicolon yourself.

    Can I combine several CSV files into one workbook?

    Yes. Choose 'One Excel file, one sheet per CSV'.

    Is my spreadsheet uploaded?

    No. The file is opened by your own browser and never leaves your device, so it is safe for payroll, customer lists and other confidential data.
