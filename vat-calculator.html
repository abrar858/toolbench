    VAT calculator

    Add VAT to a price or take VAT off a VAT-inclusive price, at the UK standard rate of 20% or any other rate.

    • Free, no sign-up
    • Runs privately in your browser
    • Updated for 2026/27

    How to use the VAT calculator

    1. Enter the amount.
    2. Check the VAT rate. It is 20% by default.
    3. Choose whether to add VAT to the amount or remove VAT from it.
    4. Read the price before VAT, the VAT and the total.

    How to add and remove VAT

    • Add VAT: multiply the net price by 1.2 for 20%. £100 + VAT = £120.
    • Remove VAT: divide the gross price by 1.2. £120 including VAT is £100 before VAT, with £20 of VAT.

    A common mistake is to take 20% off the gross price. 20% of £120 is £24, which gives the wrong answer of £96.

    UK VAT rates

    The standard rate is 20%. A reduced rate of 5% applies to some goods and services, such as home energy, and some items are zero-rated, such as most food and children's clothes. Check GOV.UK for the rate that applies to what you sell.

    Quick reference

    CalculationExample at 20%
    Add VAT: × 1.2£50 → £60.00
    Remove VAT: ÷ 1.2£84 → £70.00 (VAT £14.00)
    VAT in a gross price: ÷ 6£84 → £14.00 VAT
    At 5%: × 1.05 or ÷ 1.05£105 → £100.00 (VAT £5.00)

    The ‘divide by 6’ shortcut works because at 20% VAT is one sixth of the price including VAT. At 5% it is one twenty-first.

    Zero-rated, exempt and outside the scope

    These sound similar but work differently. Zero-rated goods, such as most food and children's clothes, are VAT-able at 0%: businesses still record them and can reclaim VAT on related costs. Exempt items, such as most insurance and some education and health services, have no VAT at all, and the business usually can't reclaim VAT on related costs. Outside the scope covers things that aren't business supplies at all. GOV.UK lists which applies to what.

    When a business must register

    A UK business must register for VAT when its VAT-taxable turnover goes over the registration threshold in any rolling 12 months. The threshold was raised to £90,000 in April 2024; check GOV.UK for the current figure before relying on it. Businesses below the threshold can register voluntarily.

    VAT on everyday purchases

    • 20% (standard): most goods and services, including adult clothing, electronics, restaurant meals and hot takeaway food.
    • 5% (reduced): domestic energy such as home gas and electricity, and children's car seats.
    • 0% (zero-rated): most food from shops, children's clothes, and books and newspapers, including e-books.

    A VAT-registered business can usually reclaim the VAT it pays on business purchases if it has a valid VAT invoice, but not on everything; for example, VAT on entertaining clients generally can't be reclaimed. HMRC's guidance on GOV.UK covers the details.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I remove 20% VAT from a price?

    Divide the price by 1.2. The calculator does this when you choose 'Remove VAT from this amount'.

    How do I add VAT at 5%?

    Change the VAT rate to 5 and choose 'Add VAT to this amount'.

    Can I use other countries' VAT rates?

    Yes. Enter any rate. Amounts are shown in pounds, but the maths is the same for any currency.

    How do I work out the VAT in a price that includes VAT?

    Divide the price by 6 at the 20% rate. For £84 including VAT, the VAT is £14 and the price before VAT is £70.
