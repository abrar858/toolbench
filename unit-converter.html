    Unit converter

    Convert between metric and imperial units for length, weight, volume, temperature, speed, area, time, pressure, energy, power, angles and data size.

    • Free, no sign-up
    • Runs privately in your browser
    • Instant results

    Data size: decimal kilo/mega/giga/tera use powers of 1,000; binary kibi/mebi/gibi/tebi use powers of 1,024. All data units here measure bytes, not bits.

    How to use the unit converter

    1. Choose what you are measuring, such as length or temperature.
    2. Enter a value.
    3. Pick the unit to convert from and to. Use Swap to reverse the conversion.

    Common conversions

    • 1 inch = 2.54 centimetres
    • 1 mile = 1.609344 kilometres
    • 1 kilogram ≈ 2.20462 pounds, and 1 stone = 14 pounds
    • 1 UK pint ≈ 568 millilitres (a US pint is smaller, about 473 ml)
    • °F = °C × 9 ÷ 5 + 32, so 20 °C = 68 °F

    Data sizes: MB or MiB?

    Kilobytes, megabytes and gigabytes use powers of 1,000. Kibibytes, mebibytes and gibibytes use powers of 1,024. That is why a 1 TB drive shows as about 931 GiB on some computers. All data units here measure bytes, not bits, so divide internet speeds in megabits by 8 to get megabytes per second.

    Categories covered

    • Length: millimetres, centimetres, metres, kilometres, inches, feet, yards and miles.
    • Weight: grams, kilograms, tonnes, ounces, pounds and stone.
    • Volume: millilitres, litres, UK pints, UK gallons, US cups and US gallons.
    • Temperature: Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin.
    • Speed, area, time, pressure, energy, power and angles, with common metric and imperial units.
    • Data size: bytes through terabytes and tebibytes.

    Results are shown to up to 10 significant figures, and very large or small values switch to scientific notation so they stay readable.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I convert Celsius to Fahrenheit?

    Multiply by 9, divide by 5 and add 32. Choose Temperature in the converter to do it instantly.

    How many kilograms are in a stone?

    One stone is 14 pounds, or about 6.35 kilograms.

    Why is my hard drive smaller than advertised?

    Drive makers use decimal gigabytes (1,000³ bytes) while some operating systems report binary gibibytes (1,024³ bytes).
