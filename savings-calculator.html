    AER calculator: convert a gross rate to AER, or back

    How to use the savings calculator

    1. Enter your starting amount.
    2. Enter how much you will add each month.
    3. Enter the interest rate (AER) and the number of years.
    4. Read the final balance, how much you paid in, the interest earned and the year-by-year table.
    5. To compare accounts, open AER calculator and enter a rate and how often interest is paid.

    How compound interest works

    Compound interest means you earn interest on your interest. The calculator converts the AER into an equivalent monthly rate, adds interest to the balance each month and then adds your monthly deposit. Over long periods, the interest can grow to more than the amount you paid in.

    Worked example

    Start with £1,000, save £100 a month at 4% AER for 10 years. You pay in £13,000 and finish with about £16,149.84, so £3,149.84 is interest.

    AER and gross rate

    Savings accounts quote two rates. The gross rate is the rate before compounding, used to work out each interest payment. The AER (annual equivalent rate) shows what you'd earn over a year once that interest is added to your balance, so accounts that pay monthly, quarterly or yearly can be compared fairly.

    AER = (1 + gross rate ÷ n)n − 1, where n is the number of interest payments a year.

    • 5% gross paid monthly is 5.12% AER.
    • 4.5% gross paid quarterly is 4.58% AER.
    • Going the other way, 4% AER paid monthly is 3.93% gross.

    When interest is paid once a year, the gross rate and the AER are the same.

    Things to keep in mind

    Real savings rates change over time, and inflation reduces what your money can buy. Interest above your Personal Savings Allowance may be taxable, while interest in an ISA is tax-free. This is a guide, not financial advice.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is AER?

    The annual equivalent rate shows what you would earn in a year with interest compounded, so accounts that pay interest at different frequencies can be compared fairly.

    How do I convert a gross rate to AER?

    Open AER calculator, enter the gross rate and how often interest is paid. For example, 5% gross paid monthly is 5.12% AER.

    How much will £100 a month grow to?

    At 4% AER with £1,000 to start, about £16,150 after 10 years. Enter your own figures to see your result.

    Does the calculator include tax?

    No. It shows growth before any tax on interest.
