    How to use the percentage calculator

    1. Use the first line to find a percentage of a number, such as 15% of 200.
    2. Use the second line to find what percentage one number is of another.
    3. Use the third line to find the percentage increase or decrease between two values.

    Percentage formulas

    • X% of Y = X ÷ 100 × Y. So 15% of 200 = 30.
    • A as a percentage of B = A ÷ B × 100. So 30 is 25% of 120.
    • Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. So a change from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase.

    A common mistake

    A rise and a fall of the same percentage do not cancel out. A £100 price that rises 10% to £110 and then falls 10% ends at £99, because the fall is 10% of the larger number.

    Everyday examples

    25% off £80£80 × 0.75£60

    Reverse percentages

    To find the price before a percentage was added, divide instead of subtracting. If a price is £120 after a 20% rise, the original was £120 ÷ 1.2 = £100, not £120 − 20% = £96. The same works for discounts: if £60 is 25% off, the full price was £60 ÷ 0.75 = £80.

    Percent versus percentage points

    When an interest rate rises from 4% to 5%, it has gone up by 1 percentage point, but by 25% in relative terms, because 1 is a quarter of 4. News reports and loan offers mix these up, so check which one is meant.

    Mental maths shortcuts

    • 10%: move the decimal point one place left. 10% of £230 is £23.
    • 5%: half of 10%. 5% of £230 is £11.50.
    • Swap them round: 8% of 50 is the same as 50% of 8, which is 4.

    Percentages that stack

    Two discounts don't add up. A coat with 20% off, then a further 10% off at the till, costs £100 × 0.8 × 0.9 = £72, which is 28% off in total, not 30%. Interest that compounds works the same way in reverse: 5% a year for two years grows £1,000 to £1,102.50, not £1,100. In a spreadsheet, to show each row as a percentage of a total in cell A10, use =A2/A$10 and format the column as a percentage.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I work out a percentage of a number?

    Divide the percentage by 100 and multiply by the number. 20% of 50 is 0.2 × 50 = 10.

    How do I calculate percentage increase?

    Take the old value from the new value, divide by the old value and multiply by 100.

    How do I add VAT as a percentage?

    Use the VAT calculator, which adds or removes VAT at 20% or any other rate.

    How do I find the original price before a percentage increase?

    Divide the new price by 1 plus the percentage as a decimal. After a 20% rise to £120, the original price was £120 ÷ 1.2 = £100.
