    BMI calculator for adults

    Calculate your body mass index using centimetres and kilograms, or feet, inches, stone and pounds.

    • Free, no sign-up
    • Runs privately in your browser
    • Instant results

    For adults aged 18 and over. Not suitable during pregnancy.

    BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (metres)². Standard adult categories are shown. For South Asian, Chinese, other Asian, Middle Eastern, Black African and African–Caribbean backgrounds, overweight starts at 23 and obesity at 27.5. BMI cannot distinguish muscle from fat and needs careful interpretation in older adults. It is a screening estimate, not a diagnosis.

    Read NHS guidance about BMI

    How to use the BMI calculator

    1. Choose metric or imperial units.
    2. Enter your height and weight.
    3. Read your BMI and its standard adult category.

    How BMI is calculated

    BMI is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres squared. For someone 175 cm tall weighing 70 kg, BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.9, which is in the healthy weight range.

    Adult BMI categories

    • Below 18.5: underweight
    • 18.5 to 24.9: healthy weight
    • 25 to 29.9: overweight
    • 30 or more: obesity

    For people of South Asian, Chinese, other Asian, Middle Eastern, Black African or African–Caribbean background, the NHS uses lower thresholds: overweight from 23 and obesity from 27.5.

    Limits of BMI

    BMI does not tell muscle from fat, so very muscular people can score high. It is not suitable for children or during pregnancy, and needs care in older adults. It is a screening number, not a diagnosis. Speak to a GP or health professional about your own health.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is a healthy BMI?

    For most adults, a BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 is classed as a healthy weight.

    Can I calculate BMI in stone and pounds?

    Yes. Choose imperial units and enter your height in feet and inches and your weight in stone and pounds.

    Is BMI accurate for athletes?

    Not always. Muscle weighs more than fat, so BMI can overestimate body fat in very muscular people.
