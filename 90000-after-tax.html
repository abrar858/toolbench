    £90,000 after tax in the UK

    On a £90,000 salary you take home £62,757.40 a year, £5,229.78 a month or £1,206.87 a week in the 2026/27 tax year.

    How the tax is worked out

    • Tax-free personal allowance: £12,570.
    • Basic rate at 20% on £37,700: £7,540.00.
    • Higher rate at 40% on £39,730: £15,892.00.
    • National Insurance at 8% on £37,700 of earnings between £12,570 and £50,270: £3,016.00.
    • National Insurance at 2% on £39,730 above £50,270: £794.60.

    A pay rise of £100 at this level would leave you about £58.00 better off, because the next pound is taxed at a combined 42%.

    With a 5% workplace pension

    Paying 5% of salary (£4,500.00 a year) into a net pay pension scheme reduces income tax to £21,632.00 and leaves take-home pay of £60,057.40 a year, or £5,004.78 a month. The pension costs you £2,700.00 a year in take-home pay because tax relief makes up the rest. Salary sacrifice schemes also save National Insurance.

    Hourly rate

    On a 37.5-hour week, £90,000 works out at about £46.15 an hour before tax, or £32.18 an hour after tax and National Insurance.

    Frequently asked questions

    How much is £90,000 a month after tax?

    In the 2026/27 tax year, £90,000 a year is £5,229.78 a month after income tax and National Insurance, assuming a standard tax code, no pension contribution and no student loan, in England, Wales or Northern Ireland.

    What tax band is a £90,000 salary in?

    £90,000 falls in the higher rate (40%) band. Each extra £1 earned is taxed at a combined rate of about 42% including National Insurance.

    How much is £90,000 an hour?

    At 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, £90,000 is about £46.15 an hour before tax and £32.18 an hour after tax and National Insurance.

    How much tax do I pay on £90,000?

    £23,432.00 income tax and £3,810.60 employee National Insurance a year: £27,242.60 in total, or 30.3% of your salary.

    Is this right if I live in Scotland?

    No. Scotland has its own income tax bands, so your income tax will differ. National Insurance is the same across the UK.
