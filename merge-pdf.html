    Merge PDF files

    Combine two or more PDFs into a single document. Put them in the order you want and download the result in seconds.

    • Free, no sign-up
    • Runs privately in your browser
    • Instant results
    ⇪Choose PDF filesor drag and drop files here · files stay on your device

      Add two or more PDFs. Use the arrows to set the order.

      How to merge PDF files

      1. Choose your PDF files, or drag and drop them onto the box.
      2. Use the arrows to put the files in the right order. Remove any you added by mistake.
      3. Press Merge PDFs. The combined file downloads as merged.pdf.

      Private PDF merging

      Most online PDF tools upload your documents to their servers. This one doesn't. The merging happens inside your browser using the open-source pdf-lib library, so contracts, bank statements, medical letters and other private documents stay on your computer or phone.

      What is kept

      Every page is copied exactly as it is: text stays selectable and searchable, images keep their quality, and page sizes and orientation are unchanged. Bookmarks and form fields from the original files may not carry over. To change the pages afterwards, use Split PDF, Rotate PDF or Delete PDF pages.

      Common uses

      Join scanned pages into one document, combine invoices or receipts for an expense claim, put a CV and cover letter into a single file for a job application, or bring together the parts of a report before sending it.

      Other ways to combine PDFs, compared

      • Mac: Preview can combine PDFs by dragging page thumbnails from one file into another's sidebar. It works, but it's fiddly with many files.
      • Windows: there's no built-in way to merge PDFs. Printing to ‘Microsoft Print to PDF’ only handles one document at a time.
      • Adobe Acrobat: the free Acrobat Reader can't combine files; that needs a paid Acrobat subscription or Adobe's online tools, which upload your files.
      • This tool: works the same on Windows, Mac, Chromebook, iPhone and Android, keeps pages exactly as they are, and never uploads anything.

      Worked example: an expense claim

      Say you have a claim form (claim.pdf, 2 pages) and three receipts saved as PDFs. Choose all four, use the arrows to put the claim form first and the receipts in date order, and press Merge PDFs. You get one 5-page merged.pdf to attach to a single email. If any receipt is a photo rather than a PDF, turn it into a PDF first with JPG to PDF.

      If something goes wrong

      • ‘Password-protected’ message: open the file in your PDF reader with its password, save or print a copy without a password, then merge that copy.
      • The merged file is large: its size is roughly the sizes of the originals added together. Run it through Compress PDF if it's too big to email.
      • Pages are different sizes: each page keeps its own size and orientation. That's normal and prints correctly.
      • Form fields or bookmarks are missing: those don't always survive merging. Fill in and save forms before merging.

      Frequently asked questions

      How do I combine PDF files into one?

      Choose the PDFs, arrange them with the arrows and press Merge PDFs. The merged file downloads straight away.

      Is there a limit on files or size?

      There is no fixed limit. Very large files depend on your device's memory; most phones and computers handle hundreds of pages comfortably.

      Can I merge password-protected PDFs?

      No. Remove the password in your PDF reader first, then merge the unlocked copy.

      Are my files uploaded?

      No. Your files are processed by your own browser and never leave your device. Nothing is sent to a server, so it works for confidential documents and you can even use it offline once the page has loaded.

      Can I merge PDFs on my phone?

      Yes. Open this page in your phone's browser, choose the PDFs from your files or downloads, and the merged file is saved to your downloads.

      Does merging reduce quality?

      No. Pages are copied as they are, so text stays sharp and searchable and images keep their resolution.
