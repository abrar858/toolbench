    50/30/20 budget planner

    Split your monthly take-home pay into needs, wants and savings using the popular 50/30/20 budgeting rule.

    • Free, no sign-up
    • Runs privately in your browser
    • Updated for 2026/27

    Needs are bills you must pay: rent, council tax, energy, food, transport, minimum debt payments. Wants are everything optional. The last 20% goes to savings or paying debt off faster.

    How to use the 50/30/20 budget planner

    1. Enter your monthly take-home pay after tax.
    2. See how much the rule suggests for needs, wants and savings or debt.
    3. Compare it with what you really spend and adjust.

    The 50/30/20 rule

    • 50% needs: rent or mortgage, council tax, energy, water, food, transport, insurance and minimum debt payments.
    • 30% wants: eating out, subscriptions, holidays, hobbies and other optional spending.
    • 20% savings and debt: an emergency fund, pension or ISA, and paying debts off faster.

    Worked example

    On £2,200 a month, the rule suggests £1,100 for needs, £660 for wants and £440 for savings and debt.

    Make it fit your life

    Housing costs in many parts of the UK mean needs take more than half of income. Treat 50/30/20 as a starting point. Even saving 5% or 10% builds a buffer over time. Not sure of your take-home pay? Use the UK take-home pay calculator first.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is 50/30/20 based on gross or net income?

    Take-home pay, after tax, National Insurance and pension.

    What if my needs are more than 50%?

    That is common. Reduce wants first, then look for savings on bills, and aim to raise the savings share over time.

    Does paying off debt count as saving?

    Extra payments above the minimum go in the 20% part. Minimum payments count as needs.
