    50/30/20 budget calculator

    Split your take-home pay into needs, wants and savings using the popular 50/30/20 budgeting rule, whether you're paid weekly, fortnightly, every 4 weeks or monthly.

    • Free, no sign-up
    • Runs privately in your browser
    • Updated for 2026/27

    By Bisma Zainab, developer of ToolsBravo · Figures checked against official sources · Reviewed 22 November 2026

    Compare with what you actually spend

    Enter what you spend in the same period as your pay.

    Needs are bills you must pay: rent, council tax, energy, food, transport, minimum debt payments. Wants are everything optional. The last 20% goes to savings or paying debt off faster.

    Add this calculator to your website

    Free to use on your own website or blog. Copy this code into your page and keep the credit link.

    How to use the 50/30/20 budget planner

    1. Enter your take-home pay after tax and choose how often you're paid.
    2. See how much the rule suggests for needs, wants and savings or debt each pay period, and the monthly and weekly equivalents.
    3. Open Compare with what you actually spend and enter your bills, wants and savings for the same period.
    4. See where you're over or under each target and how much is left unallocated.

    The 50/30/20 rule

    • 50% needs: rent or mortgage, council tax, energy, water, food, transport, insurance and minimum debt payments.
    • 30% wants: eating out, subscriptions, holidays, hobbies and other optional spending.
    • 20% savings and debt: an emergency fund, pension or ISA, and paying debts off faster.

    Worked example

    On £2,200 a month, the rule suggests £1,100 for needs, £660 for wants and £440 for savings and debt.

    If you're paid weekly or fortnightly

    Choose how often you're paid and the split is shown for each payday, which is easier to stick to than a monthly figure. On £500 a week, that's £250 for needs, £150 for wants and £100 for savings each week.

    Bills are often monthly, so the calculator also shows the monthly equivalent. There are 52 weeks in a year, so £500 a week is £26,000 a year or £2,166.67 a month (£1,083.33, £650 and £433.33). Don't multiply weekly pay by 4: that misses about four weeks of pay over a year. Four-weekly pay works the same way, with 13 paydays a year.

    Compare with your real spending

    Open Compare with what you actually spend and add up your spending for the same period as your pay. On £2,200 a month with £1,250 of bills, £500 of wants and £200 saved, needs are £150 over target at 57%, wants are £160 under, and £250 is left unallocated. Saving that too would put 20% towards savings and debt, right on the rule.

    For needs, count everything you must pay each month: rent or mortgage, council tax, energy, water, broadband and phone, food, travel, insurance, childcare and minimum debt payments. Bank statements for the last two or three months give the most honest figure.

    Make it fit your life

    Housing costs in many parts of the UK mean needs take more than half of income. Treat 50/30/20 as a starting point. Even saving 5% or 10% builds a buffer over time. Not sure of your take-home pay? Use the UK take-home pay calculator first.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is 50/30/20 based on gross or net income?

    Take-home pay, after tax, National Insurance and pension.

    How do I use 50/30/20 if I'm paid weekly?

    Choose Weekly and enter your weekly take-home pay. For example, £500 a week gives £250 for needs, £150 for wants and £100 for savings each week.

    What if my needs are more than 50%?

    That is common. Reduce wants first, then look for savings on bills, and aim to raise the savings share over time.

    Does paying off debt count as saving?

    Extra payments above the minimum go in the 20% part. Minimum payments count as needs.

    Is it the 50/30/20 or the 20/30/50 rule?

    They're the same rule written in a different order: 50% of take-home pay for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and debt. Some people put saving first, which is why you may see 20/30/50.

    How do I make a monthly budget?

    Start with your monthly take-home pay, list your bills and essentials, then your optional spending, and see what's left for saving. Enter the totals under Compare with what you actually spend to check them against 50/30/20.
