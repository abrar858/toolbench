    PDF to Excel converter

    Turn tables in a PDF, such as bank statements, invoices and reports, into an Excel spreadsheet you can sort, filter and total.

    • Free, no sign-up
    • Runs privately in your browser
    • Instant results
    ⇪Choose a PDF fileor drag and drop a file here · files stay on your device

    Choose a PDF to start. Works with PDFs that contain real text, such as bank statements, invoices and reports.

    How to convert PDF to Excel

    1. Choose a PDF file.
    2. Pick one sheet per page or all pages in one sheet. Optionally limit the pages, for example 2-4.
    3. Leave number conversion on so amounts like 1,234.50 and £12 become real numbers Excel can add up.
    4. Press Convert to Excel, check the preview and download the .xlsx file.

    How the conversion works

    PDFs don't store tables, only pieces of text and where they sit on the page. The converter reads those positions with pdf.js, groups text on the same line into rows, and lines up the columns across the whole page. That works well for statements, price lists and reports with clear columns.

    Getting the best result

    • Use the original PDF from your bank or software, not a photo or scan.
    • If a page has several tables or a long text header, convert just the pages you need.
    • Cells that wrap onto two lines in the PDF may appear as two rows; merge them in Excel afterwards.

    Scanned PDFs

    A scanned PDF is a picture with no real text, so there is nothing to put into cells. If the converter reports no text, run the document through OCR (text recognition) first. Many phone scanner apps can do this.

    Worked example: a bank statement

    1. Download the statement from online banking as a PDF. Don't use a scan or screenshot.
    2. Convert it here with All pages in one sheet, so every transaction ends up in one list.
    3. In Excel or Google Sheets, delete the heading rows above the transactions on each page.
    4. Add a Category column, then use a filter or pivot table to total your spending by category.

    Amounts like 2,450.00, £12.50 and (35.00) become real numbers, with brackets turned into negatives, so totals work straight away.

    Tidying up in Excel

    • Dates stay as text so that 01/03/2026 isn't misread as 3 January. To turn them into dates, select the column and use Data → Text to Columns → Date: DMY.
    • ‘CR’ and ‘DR’ markers after amounts are kept as text. Use Find and Replace to remove them, then put a minus sign on debits.
    • A description wrapped onto two lines in the PDF can become two rows. Join them by hand, or filter out rows with no amount.

    What converts well

    • Works well: bank and credit card statements, invoices, price lists, timetables and reports with clear columns.
    • Needs tidying: pages with several tables side by side, or long descriptions that wrap onto two lines.
    • Won't work: scanned pages and photos, which have no text, and charts, which are drawings rather than numbers.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I convert a bank statement PDF to Excel?

    Download the statement as a PDF from online banking, choose it here, keep 'Turn numbers into real numbers' on and press Convert to Excel.

    Will the formatting be the same as the PDF?

    The text and columns are kept; fonts, colours and borders are not. You get a clean sheet ready to sort and total.

    Does it work with Google Sheets?

    Yes. Upload the .xlsx file to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets.

    Is my PDF uploaded?

    No. The PDF is read by your own browser and never leaves your device.

    Can I convert a scanned bank statement?

    Not directly. A scan has no text to read. Run it through OCR first, or download the statement again as a PDF from online banking.
