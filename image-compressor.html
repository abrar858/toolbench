    Image compressor

    Make photos much smaller for email, websites and upload limits, or set a target such as ‘under 100 KB’ and let the tool find the best quality that fits.

    • Instant results
    ⇪Choose imagesor drag and drop files here · files stay on your device

    Each image gets the highest quality that fits. If that isn't enough, it is made smaller in width too. Useful for visa, passport and job application forms.

    Choose one or more images.

    How to compress an image

    1. Choose one or more images.
    2. Pick JPG or WebP, a maximum width and a quality. 70% quality at 1920 px suits most uses.
    3. Press Compress and see the size saving for each image.
    4. Download each image or all of them as a ZIP.

    How to get the smallest files

    Two settings matter most. Reducing the width has the biggest effect: a 12-megapixel phone photo resized to 1920 px wide is around a quarter of the pixels. Lowering the quality to 60–75% removes detail you are unlikely to notice. WebP files are usually 25–35% smaller than JPG at the same quality.

    Typical results

    A 4 MB phone photo usually compresses to between 250 KB and 600 KB at 1920 px and 70% quality. Screenshots and graphics saved as PNG can shrink even more when converted, but use PNG-friendly settings if you need sharp text.

    Use cases

    Fit photos under email attachment limits, meet upload size limits on job and government forms, and speed up websites and online shops, where smaller images load faster and help search rankings.

    Compress to a target size

    Many visa, passport, job and university application forms reject photos over a limit such as 100 KB or 200 KB. Choose Aim for a file size, pick the limit, and each image is saved at the highest quality that fits. If even low quality isn't enough, the tool also reduces the width step by step. In testing, a 3.3 MB photo came out at 99 KB.

    How compression works

    JPG and WebP are ‘lossy’ formats: they make files smaller by discarding detail the eye is unlikely to notice, and the quality setting controls how much. At 70% or above, most photos look the same as the original on screen. Below about 50%, you start to see blocky patches in smooth areas like sky. PNG is lossless, which is why PNG photos are so big; saving them as JPG or WebP makes the biggest difference.

    Privacy bonus: location data removed

    Phone photos often contain hidden details such as the camera model, date and the exact GPS location where they were taken. Compressed images are redrawn from scratch, so that information isn't copied into the new file. That makes this a simple way to remove location data before posting photos online.

    JPG or WebP?

    WebP files are usually noticeably smaller than JPG at the same quality, and every current major browser shows them, so WebP is ideal for your own website. Some older software and many upload forms still only accept JPG or PNG, though. For anything you're attaching to a form or sending to someone else, JPG is the safe choice.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I reduce a photo to under 100 KB?

    Choose the photo, set ‘How to compress’ to ‘Aim for a file size’, pick 100 KB and press Compress.

    How do I reduce the size of a photo?

    Choose the photo, keep the default settings and press Compress. Download the smaller copy.

    How do I compress an image to under 100 KB?

    Choose a maximum width of 800 px and a quality of around 60%. Check the size shown and lower the quality if needed.

    Will compression reduce quality?

    Slightly, but at 70% or higher most people can't see the difference on screen.

    Are my files uploaded?

    No. Your files are processed by your own browser and never leave your device. Nothing is sent to a server, so it works for confidential documents and you can even use it offline once the page has loaded.

    Does compressing remove the photo's location?

    Yes. The new file is drawn from the pixels only, so GPS location and camera details from the original aren't included.
